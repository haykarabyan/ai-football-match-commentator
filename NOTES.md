# Gemini model exploration notes

Hackathon scratch notes, 2026-10-03. Everything below is **observed on this
machine against the real API**, not copied from docs. Latencies are single
samples from a laptop on conference wifi — treat them as orders of magnitude,
not benchmarks.

SDK: `google-genai` 2.28.0, Python 3.14.7.

---

## gemini-3.8-flash-tts / gemini-3.8-flash-lite-tts

**Worked.** Voice Design is the interesting part: you describe a voice in prose,
get back a reusable `voice_...` id.

| step | time |
|---|---|
| `voices.create()` × 3 | 21.2 s / 16.5 s / 13.2 s |
| synthesis, ~4–8 s of speech | 3.8 – 5.7 s |
| same Armenian line on flash-lite-tts | 4.7 s vs 5.5 s on flash-tts |

- All three designed personas were clearly distinguishable — the elderly
  Armenian grandmother, sports commentator and Swedish nurse are not subtle
  variations of one voice. The `style` annotation on top of the persona does
  real work.
- **Armenian works well**, including in a voice designed with
  `language_code: "hy-AM"`. The grandmother persona carried over to Armenian
  text with the accent intact.
- Voice Design returns a free **preview sample** on creation
  (`created_voice.sample_audio`) — 10–28 s of the voice talking. Saved as
  `voice_preview_*.wav`. Cheaper than synthesizing your own audition line.

### Surprises
- **`response_format.sample_rate` is ignored.** Asked for
  `{"mime_type": "audio/l16", "sample_rate": 16000}` and got back
  `audio/l16; rate=24000; channels=1`. Output is 24 kHz, full stop. If you
  need 16 kHz (e.g. to feed Live) you resample yourself.
- Voice design is slow enough (13–21 s) that you want to create voices ahead
  of time and cache the ids, never inside a request path.

### Limits hit
- Stored voices: 200 per project, 1-year TTL. Each run of
  `01_tts_voices.py` burns 3, so don't leave it on a loop.

## gemini-3.1-flash-lite-image (Nano Banana 2 Lite)

**Worked**, after one fix.

| generation | time |
|---|---|
| mic | 10.2 s |
| waveform | 6.6 s |
| kitchen | 7.0 s |
| mean | 7.9 s |

### Surprise / docs bug
The docs example writes `generated_image.png` and the model **rejects**
`image/png`:

```
400 The value 'image/png' is not supported for 'response_format.mime_type'.
    Supported values: 'image/jpeg'.
```

JPEG only on this model. Files came back 630–890 KB at 16:9.

### Limits
- 1K resolution only (`image_size: "1K"`); no Google Search grounding, no
  multi-reference input. For a voice app this is fine for mood boards, but
  ~8 s is too slow to sit inside a conversational turn.

## gemini-3.8-flash

**Worked.** Same prompt at two thinking levels:

| thinking_level | latency | thought tokens | output tokens |
|---|---|---|---|
| low | 4.0 s | **0** | 244 |
| high | 18.5 s | 2753 | 140 |

### Surprises
- `low` spent **literally zero** thinking tokens. It's not "a little
  thinking", it's off. So `low` is the latency floor and `medium` is the first
  level where you pay anything.
- **High thinking produced a shorter answer.** Low listed 4 tool calls
  including a `get_current_datetime()` step; high collapsed it to 2 and
  justified why. More thinking bought decisiveness, not verbosity — the
  opposite of what I expected.
- 4.6× latency for that. At 18.5 s this is a background brain, not something
  you put in a voice turn. `low` at 4 s is still too slow to sit between a
  user's question and a spoken reply.

## gemini-3.5-transcribe (batch)

**Worked, and this was the most pleasant surprise of the lot.** Fed it the
18.2 s two-speaker dialogue generated by multi-speaker TTS.

| step | time |
|---|---|
| `files.upload()` | 2.5 s |
| transcribe + diarize + word timestamps | 3.3 s |
| transcribe + custom vocabulary | 2.8 s |

- Diarization was **perfect** — 4 turns, `spk:0` / `spk:1`, no bleed.
- 43 `word_info` annotations, each with `speaker`, `start_offset`,
  `end_offset` as strings like `"0.200s"`, `"2s"`.
- It correctly rendered spoken "three point eight" as **"3.8"** and
  "Gemini three point five Transcribe" as "Gemini 3.5 transcribe".

### Surprises
- Offsets are **strings with a unit suffix**, and the suffix is dropped for
  whole numbers (`"2s"`, `"15s"`). Parse accordingly.
- In verbatim+diarization mode, `output_text` concatenates speaker turns
  **with no separating space**: `...live?Yes, and the...`. The per-word
  annotations are the real output; treat `output_text` as a preview.
- The `custom_vocabulary` pass produced better casing ("Gemini 3.8 Live",
  "Transcribe") than the verbatim pass.

### Limits hit
- `custom_vocabulary` **cannot** be combined with `diarization_mode` or
  `timestamp_granularities` — that's why `04_transcribe_batch.py` makes two
  calls. Same for `mode: "smart"`.
- 1 h audio max, 30 min with advanced features; up to 8 speakers.

## gemini-3.8-live

**Worked.** Connects in **0.4–0.9 s**, which is the headline number for a
voice app.

Audio-in timeline, hold-to-talk, with the deliberately-slow 6 s tool:

| event | t |
|---|---|
| connected | 0.5 s |
| button released (`activity_end`) | 4.5 s |
| input transcript | 4.8 s |
| first spoken words | 5.3 s |
| tool call arrives | 6.3 s |
| tool returns (6 s sleep) | 12.4 s |
| spoken answer | 12.9 s |

### Surprises
- **It does not actually talk through the tool call.** It speaks a filler
  ("Sure thing! Let me pull up the hackathon schedule…") *before* issuing the
  call, then goes **completely silent for the full 6 s**, then answers. The
  filler is pre-emptive, not concurrent. If you want continuous speech over a
  slow tool on this model, you have to generate it yourself.
- `FunctionResponse.scheduling = INTERRUPT` is accepted here.
- `scheduling` lives as a **top-level field** on `types.FunctionResponse` in
  the Python SDK, not nested inside the `response` dict as the docs example
  shows.

## gemini-3.8-live-extended-thinking

**Worked, and this is the one that behaves as advertised.**

| event | t |
|---|---|
| first spoken filler | **1.0 s** |
| `interaction_status = IN_PROGRESS` | 3.7 s |
| tool call arrives | 4.7 s |
| tool returns | 9.7 s |
| answer | 10.9 s |
| `interaction_status = IDLE` | 17.6 s |

- It speaks **4 s before the tool call is even issued** — genuine
  conversational cover, not a pre-canned filler.
- Given both tools it **chained them unprompted**: called `get_time`, got
  12:33, called `lookup_hackathon_schedule`, then reasoned *"it's 12:33, so
  there are no sessions right now; coming up next at 1:00 PM is the Gemini
  Live API workshop."* The plain Live model just read the first schedule line
  back.
- Word delivery is visibly slower (~1 s between transcript fragments vs
  ~0.25 s on plain Live). You can hear it thinking.

### Limitation hit — hard failure
Sending `FunctionResponse.scheduling` on this model **kills the socket**:

```
websockets.exceptions.ConnectionClosedError: received 1007 (invalid frame payload data)
Function response scheduling is not supported for this model.
```

Not a warning, not an ignored field — a 1007 close that takes the session
down. `server/app.py` only sets `scheduling` when the model is plain
`gemini-3.8-live`.

## gemini-3.5-live-translate-preview

**Worked** in both directions tested.

| pair | connect | first translated audio |
|---|---|---|
| en → hy | 0.63 s | 4.1 s |
| hy → sv | 0.46 s | 3.7 s |
| en → sv | 0.53 s | 3.9 s |

- Armenian (`hy`) and Swedish (`sv`) are both supported, as source *and*
  target.
- ~6 s of input produced ~730 KB of 24 kHz audio back.
- You get `input_transcription` and `output_transcription` side by side, which
  makes a nice two-column UI almost free.

### Surprises
- **There is no `source_language_code`.** `TranslationConfig` has exactly two
  fields: `target_language_code` and `echo_target_language`. The source is
  auto-detected. The "speak" dropdown in the Translate tab is therefore
  cosmetic — I left it in because it's useful to tell yourself which language
  to speak, but it is not sent anywhere.
- Translation is chunk-by-chunk and slightly behind the speaker, so the
  output is grammatically choppy mid-utterance (`"Hej. Jag" / " testar
  live-transkriberingen" / " och"`). It reads fine once the turn completes.

### Limits
- Audio input only, no text. ~100 ms chunks as documented.

## gemini-3.5-transcribe-live

**Worked.**

| event | t |
|---|---|
| connected | 0.5–1.2 s |
| first interim hypothesis | 1.7 s |
| final transcript (5.9 s clip) | 6.9 s |

Auto language detection genuinely works — fed it English, Armenian and
Swedish back to back in one session and all three came out right, with no
language codes configured (`language_codes: []`).

### Surprises
- **`Transcription.language_code` is always `None`.** The field exists in the
  SDK and the model clearly detects the language, but it is never populated.
  So "show the detected language on screen" is **not** directly possible.
  Workaround in the Captions tab: a separate `gemini-3.5-flash-lite` call
  labels each finalized caption. Costs ~2.1–2.4 s, runs out of band, so the
  caption appears immediately and the badge fills in late. It was accurate on
  all three languages.
- **Interim transcripts are cumulative, not deltas.** Each
  `interim_input_transcription` is the whole sentence so far, so you assign
  rather than append.
- It mis-transcribed Armenian *հաքաթոնում* ("at the hackathon") as
  *հայերենում* ("in Armenian") — a good argument for `custom_vocabulary`,
  which the Captions tab exposes as a text box.

### Limits
- 10 min continuous streaming.

## Cross-cutting gotchas (these cost the most time)

### 1. Automatic VAD never ends a push-to-talk turn
The worst failure of the day, because it fails **silently**. Streaming audio
and then stopping produced: connection fine, no transcript, no response, no
audio, **no error**.

Automatic VAD decides the turn ended by hearing trailing silence **in the
stream**. A hold-to-talk button stops sending audio the instant you release,
so that silence never arrives and the turn never closes. Appending 2 s of
zero-filled PCM made it work immediately — which confirmed the diagnosis but
is a hack.

The real fix, now in `server/app.py` for the Live and Translate tabs:

```python
realtime_input_config=types.RealtimeInputConfig(
    automatic_activity_detection=types.AutomaticActivityDetection(disabled=True),
)
```

then `activity_start` on button down and `activity_end` on button up. Works
with zero trailing silence.

### 2. `audio_stream_end` is not a general turn-ender
It **does** finalize a turn on `gemini-3.5-transcribe-live` (Captions tab
relies on it) and does **nothing** on `gemini-3.8-live`. Don't assume it
generalizes.

### 3. `session.receive()` ends at every turn boundary
It's not a connection-lifetime iterator. `async for msg in session.receive()`
completes when the turn completes; you must re-enter it:

```python
while not ws.closed:
    async for message in session.receive():
        ...
```
Without the outer loop the first reply arrives and the session then appears
to hang forever.

### 4. `load_dotenv()` searches from the script, not the cwd
With no argument it walks up from the calling file's directory. A script in
a different folder won't find your project `.env` even when you run it from
the project root. All scripts here pass the path explicitly.

### 5. Session limits — not reached
Documented: **15 min** audio-only, **2 min** with video, **~10 min**
connection lifetime, **10 min** live transcription. My longest session was
~51 s, so **I did not hit any of these and cannot confirm the behaviour at
the boundary.** The Live chat tab enables sliding-window context compression
to push past 15 min, but that is untested here. If you demo for more than a
couple of minutes, add session resumption before you trust it.

## What this suggests for a voice-first app

- **Latency budget is the whole design.** Live connects in <1 s and starts
  speaking in ~1 s (extended thinking) — but `gemini-3.8-flash` at
  `thinking_level: low` is 4 s and the image model is 8 s. Anything that isn't
  the Live model has to happen *off* the conversational path.
- **`gemini-3.8-live-extended-thinking` is the one worth building on** if your
  app does anything slow. It covers latency with speech by design, and it
  reasoned across two tool results without being told to. Cost: visibly
  slower delivery and no `scheduling` support.
- **Plain `gemini-3.8-live` needs you to own the silence.** It will not fill
  a 6 s gap for you.
- **Transcribe batch is good enough to be a product on its own** — 3.3 s for
  18 s of audio with clean diarization and word timing.
- **Armenian is well supported across TTS, translate and transcribe**, which
  was the open question going in. Custom vocabulary is worth wiring up for
  proper nouns.
